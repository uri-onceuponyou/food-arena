FOOD ARENA — CANONICAL BLIND REVIEW RUBRIC, v1

Pass this text to the critic VERBATIM. `tools/review.mjs --rubric canonical` reads this
file and copies it into the packet as RUBRIC.txt, so a score is always stored next to the
exact question that produced it.

WHY THIS EXISTS: identical sheets score 5.0 under "overall visual quality" and 3.0 under
"character design and rendering only", deterministically, with the reference side
unmoved. The rubric is worth 2.0 points — larger than any change this project has ever
moved a score by — and until now no round wrote its rubric down, so no two scores were
ever on the same scale. Do not edit this file to adjust a round. Editing it starts a new
series; bump the version and say so.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THE BRIEF GIVEN TO THE CRITIC

You are shown one sheet containing two panels, labelled A and B. Each panel is a single
frame from a mobile action game.

Score EACH panel independently, 0-10, on this one question:

  How close is this frame to a screenshot from a fully released, professionally
  produced mobile action game?

That is a whole-frame judgement of finished visual quality as a paying player would see
it in an app-store listing: art direction, colour, lighting, readability, composition,
character craft, environment craft and interface polish, all together and weighted as
they strike you. Do not score gameplay, mechanics or fun. Do not give credit for
apparent effort, budget or stage of development.

Anchors, so the scale means the same thing in every round:

  10  best in class for this genre; nothing to fix
   8  shipped and competitive with the leading live titles in this genre
   6  shipped, but visibly weaker than the genre leaders
   4  clearly unfinished; would not pass as a flagship store screenshot
   2  a prototype or a greybox
   0  broken or unreadable

Rules:
  * Score both panels on their own merits. You are NOT required to pick a loser: if both
    look shipped, give both high scores, and if both look unfinished, give both low ones.
  * Score only what is in the pixels. Ignore anything you have been told elsewhere about
    this repository, its quality bar, which panel might be whose, or what any panel is
    "supposed" to be. If you think you recognise a game, that recognition must not move
    the number.
  * Panels may differ in aspect ratio, resolution and sharpness because they come from
    different sources. Judge the work, not the encode.

Then, for EACH panel, in one sentence: the single change that would most raise that
panel's score. Say WHAT YOU SEE that is wrong. Do not guess at a cause in the code, the
lighting rig or the art pipeline — a named symptom is useful, a guessed mechanism has
repeatedly been wrong here.

Return exactly this and nothing else:

  A: <score 0-10> — <one sentence: the change that would most raise panel A's score>
  B: <score 0-10> — <one sentence: the change that would most raise panel B's score>
